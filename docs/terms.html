What Tempra does to your Mac, stated plainly

Tempra manages other processes on your computer. Depending on the rules you create, it may suspend and resume them, lower their scheduling priority, hide them, or quit them. Doing this requires permissions that macOS treats seriously, and for some operations an administrator helper that you are asked to install.

You are responsible for the rules you set. Pausing an application stops it from working until it is resumed. If you pause something that is mid-download, mid-sync, mid-render, or mid-backup, that work does not continue while it is paused, and some applications handle being suspended better than others. Tempra never touches a fixed list of system-critical processes, and it holds off on applications that are playing audio, but it cannot know what every application on your Mac is in the middle of.
